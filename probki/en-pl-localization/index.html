Plannook in Polish: website, app and checkout

A localization sample for a fictional SaaS app entering the Polish market. Three typical jobs on one page: marketing copy, app strings with Polish plurals and gendered verbs, and a billing screen with Polish prices, VAT and legal wording. Each line shows the English source, my Polish version and why it is not word for word.

Plannook is a fictional shift-scheduling app (name checked in Google and the Polish company registers KRS and CEIDG on 28.11.2026). The English source, prices and numbers were written for this sample.

1. Website: transcreation, not word for word

The goal is copy that reads as if it was written in Polish, with the same promise as the English page.

EnglishStop chasing timesheets.
PolishGodziny pracy zespołu policzą się same.
WhyWord for word («Przestań ganiać za listami obecności») sounds like a reproach to the reader. Polish B2B headlines more often promise the result, so this one says what the app does for them.
EnglishPlannook builds your rota, tracks hours and exports payroll-ready reports in minutes.
PolishPlannook układa grafik, liczy przepracowane godziny i w kilka minut przygotowuje zestawienie dla biura rachunkowego.
WhyA rota is a «grafik» in Polish cafés and shops. Small Polish firms often hand payroll to an outside accounting office (biuro rachunkowe), so the report is named after who reads it.
EnglishStart free trial
PolishWypróbuj za darmo
WhyThe literal «Rozpocznij bezpłatny okres próbny» is 33 characters and breaks the button on phones. This is 17, with the same meaning.
EnglishNo credit card required.
PolishBez podawania karty płatniczej.
WhyUnder a button, a short phrase without a verb reads naturally in Polish. «Karta kredytowa» would be too narrow: most cards in Poland are debit cards, and «karta płatnicza» covers both.
EnglishTrusted by 2,000+ small teams
PolishWybrało go już ponad 2000 małych zespołów
WhyNo comma in Polish numbers: four digits are written together, from five digits with a space (12 500). «Zaufany przez» would be a calque, so the line says who chose it.

2. App strings: plurals, gender and length

English needs two plural forms. Polish needs three, plus one for fractions. Keys and placeholders stay exactly as in your file.

en.json (line breaks added for reading)

{
  "shifts.count": "{count, plural,
    one {# shift}
    other {# shifts}}",
  "leave.approved":
    "{name} approved your leave request",
  "clock.in": "Clock in",
  "clock.out": "Clock out",
  "shift.when": "Mon, Dec 1 · 9:00 AM – 5:00 PM"
}

pl.json (line breaks added for reading)

{
  "shifts.count": "{count, plural,
    one {# zmiana}
    few {# zmiany}
    many {# zmian}
    other {# zmiany}}",
  "leave.approved": "{gender, select,
    female {{name} zatwierdziła Twój wniosek urlopowy}
    male {{name} zatwierdził Twój wniosek urlopowy}
    other {Wniosek urlopowy zatwierdzony przez: {name}}}",
  "clock.in": "Rozpocznij zmianę",
  "clock.out": "Zakończ zmianę",
  "shift.when": "pon., 1 grudnia, 9:00–17:00"
}
  • 1 zmianaone
  • 2 zmianyfew
  • 5 zmianmany
  • 12 zmianmany
  • 21 zmianmany
  • 22 zmianyfew
  • 1,5 zmianyother
IssuePlurals
Polish5 zmian, not «5 zmiany»
WhyIf a file has only «one» and «other», the app shows «5 zmiany», a mistake every Polish user notices. All four ICU categories are filled in and checked on 1, 2, 5, 12, 21, 22 and 1,5.
IssueGender
Polishzatwierdził / zatwierdziła
WhyThe Polish past tense shows whether the person is a man or a woman. If the app knows it, ICU «select» picks the form; if not, the neutral line is used. I flag such keys to your developer instead of guessing.
IssueLength
PolishRozpocznij zmianę (17) vs Clock in (8)
WhyPolish text is often noticeably longer than English. I check every button against its real width and keep a short option ready for tight spots («Start» / «Koniec»).
IssueDates and time
Polishpon., 1 grudnia, 9:00–17:00
Why24-hour clock, day before month, month names in lower case and in the genitive («grudnia»). If dates come from code, I point to the Polish locale (pl-PL) instead of hard-coding text.
Issue«Twój» or «twój»
PolishTwój wniosek
WhyA capital «Twój» is a polite convention from letters; many apps use lower case. Either is correct. I agree one style with you, write it in the glossary and keep it everywhere.

English£12 … Prices exclude VAT
Polish49 zł netto … 23% VAT
WhyBusiness plans in Poland are usually shown net («netto») with VAT on top; the standard rate is 23%. «zł» goes after the number, with a decimal comma when needed: 49,00 zł.
EnglishVAT number, Postcode
PolishNIP, Kod pocztowy (00-000)
WhyPolish companies know their tax ID as NIP (the EU VAT number is the same digits with PL in front). The postcode format is 00-000, so the hint shows it.
EnglishTerms of Service
PolishRegulamin
WhyPolish law requires an online service to set a «regulamin» and make it available free of charge before the contract, and that is the word users look for. «Warunki korzystania» would be a calque. GDPR is «RODO» in Polish.
EnglishI agree to the Terms of Service and Privacy Policy
PolishAkceptuję Regulamin. Jak przetwarzamy dane: Polityka prywatności
WhyUnder RODO (GDPR) a privacy policy is information, not something users accept, so Polish checkouts ask to accept only the Regulamin and link the policy next to it. A combined checkbox is a common calque that Polish lawyers flag.
EnglishSubscribe
PolishZamawiam z obowiązkiem zapłaty
WhyFor consumers, Polish law requires the order button to say clearly that ordering means paying; this is the wording the law names (or an equivalent). For business-only plans a shorter button may be possible, but sole traders can still be protected as consumers, so this is a point for your lawyer.

5. How I work on a localization job

  1. Glossary and style first: «Ty» or «Pan/Pani», capital «Twój» or not, which names stay in English.
  2. Your files, your format: JSON, XLIFF, PO, CSV or text from Figma. Keys, placeholders and HTML tags stay untouched.
  3. Checked in context: on screenshots or a staging link, every button at its real width, plurals tested with real numbers.
  4. Questions instead of guesses: anything unclear goes to a short list for you, with the key and two options.
  5. Legal texts: I translate the Regulamin and privacy policy and mark points that need a Polish lawyer's decision; I do not give legal advice.
