What should I do about an electrical problem in Austin right now?

Turn off the affected breaker if you can do it safely, and call (555) 012-4788 (24/7) to be connected with an independent licensed electrician serving Travis County. If the whole street is dark, the outage is on the Austin Energy side: report it at 512-322-9100 or on its outage map. Smoke or flames: leave the house and call 911 first. Before work starts, check the electrician’s TDLR license and get the price in writing.

Austin has a mix of older homes, remodels, dense apartments, and newer high-load electrical needs. About three in ten homes were built before 1980, and Central Texas heat can make AC and panel issues show up fast. If the symptom involves burning odor, buzzing, warm outlets, repeated trips, or water near wiring, call first and use the outage map only as part of utility sorting out.

Repeated breaker trips need a call

A single trip after a heavy load can be normal. A breaker that trips again after you reset it is telling you the circuit is overloaded or something on it is failing. Leave it off and call before resetting a third time.

Call this number before resetting the breaker again.

Water and electricity are the one combination where waiting is the wrong move. A wet panel area, sparking from an outdoor outlet, or a GFCI that refuses to reset should stay off until an electrician has looked at it.

Austin Electrical Questions, Answered

Why does my breaker keep tripping in my Austin home?
A tripping breaker may mean an overloaded circuit, faulty appliance, damaged wiring, moisture, or a breaker protecting the home from a real fault. Austin's mix of older housing, remodels, and high cooling demand can make repeated trips more common during heavy use. Unplug what changed recently if it is safe, then leave the breaker off if it trips again. Do not keep forcing resets. Call this number if the trip repeats, affects a major appliance, or comes with heat, odor, or buzzing.
What should I do if there is water near an outlet, panel, or electrical cord in Austin?
Water near electrical equipment is urgent because shock and hidden damage are possible. Austin has a real flood and storm history, so this symptom belongs on the urgent list, not a wait-and-see list. Stay away from the wet area, avoid touching plugs or breakers while standing in water, and shut power off only if you can do it safely from a dry location. Do not use towels, fans, or extension cords around energized wet equipment. Call this number immediately.
Should I call if an outlet feels warm or smells like burning plastic?
Yes. Warmth, odor, discoloration, or a melted plug can point to overheating, a loose connection, or damaged wiring. In Austin homes, older circuits and modern plug loads can overlap, especially in remodeled spaces or rooms with many devices. Stop using the outlet, unplug only if the plug is cool and safe to touch, and keep the area clear. Do not replace the device as a guess. Call this number right away if heat or odor is present.
Why do lights dim when the AC starts in Austin?
A small momentary dip can happen when a large motor starts, but strong dimming, repeated flicker, or dimming across several rooms may signal voltage drop, loose connections, overloaded circuits, or equipment trouble. Austin heat puts long seasonal demand on cooling equipment, so timing matters. Watch whether it happens only with the AC, but do not open the panel or upsize breakers yourself. Call if the dimming is severe, new, or paired with breaker trips.
How can I tell whether a partial outage is Austin Energy or my house?
Start by checking whether neighbors are affected and by using the outage map or the utility name shown on your bill. Austin Energy serves much of the area; check your bill to confirm. If only part of your home is out, lights are unusually bright or dim, or appliances are behaving strangely, stop using sensitive electronics. Call this number because a home-side partial outage can involve a serious connection problem.
Are older Austin homes more likely to need electrical troubleshooting?
Older homes can have outdated wiring methods, ungrounded outlets, undersized circuits, or panels that were not planned for today's loads. Austin has a meaningful share of homes built before 1980, so this is a fair local concern. A safe check is to note symptoms like two-prong outlets, frequent trips, flickering, or warm switches. Do not add adapters, daisy-chain power strips, or ignore nuisance trips. Call if older wiring symptoms appear with modern appliances, remodel work, or heat.
Do I need electrical help before adding an EV charger, hot tub, or major appliance?
A new major load can expose limits in the panel, service, or existing circuits. In Austin, remodeling and high-load upgrades often happen alongside older housing, so the question is practical before equipment is installed. Check the appliance requirements and your panel label, but do not assume an open breaker space means capacity is available. Call this number if the project is urgent because equipment is already installed, tripping, or creating heat or flicker.
What should I do if my GFCI will not reset in an Austin kitchen, bath, garage, or outdoor area?
A GFCI that will not reset may be detecting moisture, a downstream fault, a failed device, or wiring trouble. In Austin, the location matters because outdoor and garage circuits can be affected by storm moisture, while kitchens and baths add appliance load and water exposure. Unplug nearby devices and try one reset if the area is dry and safe. Do not tape the button down, bypass protection, or swap parts blindly. Call if it will not reset or trips again.
