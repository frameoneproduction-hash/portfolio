Plano Electrical Questions, Answered

After a Plano storm, how do I know whether a partial outage is a utility issue or a home electrical problem?
A partial outage may come from the utility side, damaged service equipment, a lost leg of power, or a problem inside your panel. Plano has a strong storm and hail signal, so symptoms after weather should be treated carefully. Check whether nearby homes are affected and use the outage map or your electric bill to confirm the utility for your address. Do not open the panel, touch meter equipment, or assume one working room means everything is safe. Call dispatch if only part of your home is out, lights are unusually bright or dim, breakers buzz, or you smell burning.
What should I do if an outlet sparks in my Plano home?
A spark can be minor when plugging in a load, but repeated sparks, loud pops, scorch marks, or heat can mean a loose connection or failing receptacle. In Plano, storm surges and heavy device use can both stress outlets and connected equipment. Stop using the outlet and unplug devices only if it is safe. Do not keep testing it, plug in a power strip, or remove the cover to inspect the wiring. Call immediately if the outlet is warm, discolored, buzzing, or paired with a burning smell.
Why does my breaker trip when the AC starts in Plano?
An AC-start breaker trip may point to a high startup draw, weak breaker, overloaded circuit, condenser issue, or panel connection problem. Plano has hot-weather load pressure and many homes with substantial electric demand. You can note whether the breaker trips instantly, after a few minutes, or only during peak heat. Do not keep cycling the AC or replace the breaker with a larger one. Call dispatch when the trip repeats, the panel buzzes, or lights dim sharply when the system starts.
Are flickering lights after lightning or hail a reason to call an electrician?
Flickering after severe weather may be a utility fluctuation, damaged service equipment, a loose connection, or a circuit issue inside the home. Plano has frequent hail reports and some lightning history, so post-storm flicker should not be dismissed if it continues. Check whether neighbors have the same issue and whether flickering affects the whole home or one area. Do not ignore flicker paired with buzzing, heat, burning smell, or appliance malfunction. Call dispatch if the flicker persists after the storm passes or seems limited to your home.
What if water gets near an outlet, breaker box, or outdoor electrical equipment in Plano?
Water near electrical equipment can create shock and fire risk, especially when outlets, panels, or exterior boxes are involved. Plano’s storm profile includes heavy-weather events, so wet electrical symptoms belong in urgent sorting out. Keep people away, avoid standing water, and shut off power only if you can do it safely from a dry location. Do not touch wet cords, panels, or outdoor outlets to test them. Call immediately if water reached electrical equipment, a GFCI will not reset, or breakers trip after rain.
Why will my GFCI outlet not reset after rain?
A GFCI that will not reset may be reacting to moisture, a ground fault, a failed device, or wiring trouble downstream. In Plano, outdoor outlets and garage circuits can be affected after storms or wind-driven rain. You can unplug connected devices and check whether the area is dry. Do not bypass the GFCI, tape the reset button down, or swap the outlet without finding the cause. Call dispatch if it will not reset, trips again, feeds outdoor equipment, or is connected to a wet area.
Should I call if I smell burning at a switch or panel but the power still works?
Yes. A burning smell can mean a heating connection, failing breaker, damaged insulation, or overloaded device even when power has not gone out. Plano’s mix of storm exposure and high household electrical demand makes odor near electrical equipment an urgent symptom. If it is safe, stop using the affected switch, outlet, or circuit and keep the area clear. Do not remove covers, spray the area, or keep using the device to see if the smell fades. Call immediately, especially with heat, buzzing, discoloration, or smoke.
Can a new appliance, EV charger, or remodel overload my Plano panel?
A new major load may expose limited capacity, crowded circuits, weak connections, or equipment that was fine before the added demand. Plano has many active, high-demand households, so load planning matters even in newer housing. Check whether breakers trip, lights dim, or existing circuits are being shared with the new equipment. Do not rely on extension cords, adapter chains, or an oversized breaker. Call dispatch if the new load creates heat, trips, flicker, or panel noise; for non-urgent planning, ask for licensed electrical guidance before installation.
