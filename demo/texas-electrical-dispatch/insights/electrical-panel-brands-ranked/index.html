Electrical Panel Brands Comparison

Texas homeowners replacing or upgrading a breaker box face a crowded field of manufacturers, and the differences matter more than aisle displays suggest. This insight compares the major residential electrical panel brands sold across Texas supply houses, looking at UL listing, recall history, and compatibility with the AFCI breaker requirements of the 2026 NEC, which Texas adopted statewide. We also examine bus-bar metallurgy, interrupting capacity ratings, and how each panel accepts an arc detector breaker or combination arc fault interrupter breaker without nuisance tripping. Key takeaway: for Texas residential service, panels supporting native arc fault breaker integration outperform legacy boxes retrofitted with adapters, particularly in older Houston, Dallas, and Austin housing stock where aluminum branch wiring and humidity-driven corrosion accelerate failure rates across budget-tier equipment lines.

Key Findings

When comparing electrical panel brands for Texas homes, four manufacturers dominate residential installations: Square D (Schneider Electric), Eaton (formerly Cutler-Hammer), Siemens, and GE/ABB.

Square D's QO line consistently ranks highest among Texas electricians for breaker box reliability. Its visible trip indicator makes a tripped breaker easy to spot, and the line is a common choice in custom builds across Austin, Dallas, and Houston metro areas. The QO AFCI breaker usually costs somewhat more than comparable Eaton or Siemens breakers, though prices vary by retailer and model.

Eaton's BR and CH series split the value tier. The CH series competes directly with QO on quality, while the BR series is a common builder-grade line in Texas tract housing. Eaton's arc fault circuit interrupter breaker received high marks following its 2019 redesign, which addressed earlier complaints about incompatibility with LED dimmers – a frequent service call in newer Texas subdivisions.

Siemens panels offer the strongest price-to-performance ratio. Their arc fault breaker meets the 2026 NEC requirements adopted statewide in Texas, including AFCI protection for kitchens and laundry areas (required since the 2014 NEC). Many Siemens load centers come with tin-plated copper bus bars, as do Square D QO and Eaton CH panels – a consideration given heavy Texas summer AC loads.

GE panels, now manufactured under ABB, warrant caution. The discontinued Federal Pacific Stab-Lok and Zinsco panels – still found in some older Texas homes – should be replaced rather than supplemented.

For arc detector breaker selection, all four brands now produce dual-function AFCI/GFCI units, but compatibility is brand-locked: a Square D QO panel requires Square D breakers, and aftermarket arc fault interrupter breaker substitutions void the UL listing. Texas homeowners replacing panels should expect total project costs between $2,400 and $4,800, with permit and inspection requirements varying by municipality. Texas follows the 2026 NEC statewide; cities such as San Antonio, Austin, and Houston may add local amendments, and licensed electricians must follow the state-adopted code in unincorporated areas too.

What It Means for Homeowners

For Texas homeowners, the panel brand stamped inside your breaker box quietly dictates safety, insurance posture, and resale value. The data points to a clear hierarchy: Square D QO and Eaton CH consistently outperform builder-grade lines on interrupting capacity and long-term reliability, while legacy Federal Pacific Stab-Lok and Zinsco panels remain documented fire hazards still found in homes built before the mid-1980s across Dallas, Houston, and San Antonio.

The practical takeaway centers on breaker technology, not just the enclosure. Under the 2026 NEC, which Texas adopted statewide effective September 1, 2026, an arc fault circuit interrupter breaker is required on most living-area circuits. If your panel cannot accept a modern AFCI breaker, arc detector breaker, or combination arc fault interrupter breaker, you are running 1990s-era protection in a 2026 grid. Pair that with Texas summer loads – where ERCOT peaks routinely push HVAC compressors past nameplate draw – and an undersized or obsolete breaker box becomes a measurable ignition risk.

Homeowners should open the panel door and photograph the label. If it reads Federal Pacific, Zinsco, Challenger, or Pushmatic, schedule a licensed electrician for evaluation, not replacement-by-default. For functional Square D, Eaton, Siemens, or GE panels, the higher-leverage upgrade is retrofitting an arc fault breaker on bedroom and living circuits, plus a whole-home surge device after the February 2021 grid event exposed how often Texas services see voltage transients.

Call a TDLR-licensed electrician immediately for: scorch marks, breakers that reset warm, buzzing, or any aluminum branch wiring landing on a non-CO/ALR-rated bus.

Texas homeowners: if your breaker box lacks AFCI breakers – the arc fault circuit interrupter breakers the 2026 NEC requires for most living-area circuits in new work – it is worth having a licensed electrician evaluate an upgrade.
