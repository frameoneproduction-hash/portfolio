Electrical Panel Upgrade Cost Guide

This guide breaks down what Texas residential homeowners can realistically expect to spend when upgrading an aging breaker box, and why modern code increasingly drives those numbers upward. We examine labor rates across major Texas metros, permit fees, and the rising material cost tied to code-mandated safety devices like the arc fault circuit interrupter breaker, often shortened to arc fault breaker, arc fault interrupter breaker, or arc detector breaker. Because the National Electrical Code now requires an afci breaker on most living-area circuits, replacement panels in Texas typically carry higher per-slot costs than a decade ago. The key takeaway: Texas homeowners upgrading from 100-amp to 200-amp service should budget for the panel, the service drop coordination with their utility, and a full set of code-compliant breakers, not hardware alone.

Key Findings

Key Findings: What Texas Homeowners Need to Know About Panel Upgrades

Residential electrical panel upgrades in major Texas metros like Houston, Dallas, Austin, and San Antonio commonly run about $2,200 to $4,500, and labor costs in rural areas are often somewhat lower. The single largest cost driver is no longer the breaker box itself, but the modern protective devices the NEC requires inside it.

AFCI requirements have expanded over several NEC cycles – most notably in 2008 (most living areas) and 2014 (kitchens and laundry areas) – and the 2026 NEC, adopted statewide in Texas, carries them forward. An arc fault circuit interrupter breaker, also marketed as an arc detector breaker, arc fault breaker, or arc fault interrupter breaker, now governs nearly every 120-volt circuit serving living spaces. Each AFCI breaker adds roughly $40-$65 to materials compared to a standard thermal-magnetic breaker, so a panel with many AFCI-protected circuits can add several hundred dollars or more in breaker costs before labor.

Three findings stand out for Texas homeowners weighing this investment:

First, panel age correlates strongly with insurance friction. Some insurers flag Federal Pacific Stab-Lok, Zinsco, and certain split-bus panels manufactured before 1990. Homes retaining these breaker boxes report policy non-renewals at rates exceeding three times the state average.

Second, service capacity demand is shifting. The drivers are measurable: heat pump conversions following 2023 IRA tax credits, Level 2 EV chargers, and induction range adoption. Homes still operating on 100-amp service face a near-certain upgrade within seven years if any two of these loads are added.

Third, combination AFCI/GFCI dual-function breakers – required in kitchens, laundry areas, and increasingly bathrooms – represent the most rapidly rising line item. With AFCI and dual-function breakers often costing $40 to $85 each, protective devices can add $1,000 or more to a fully equipped panel.

Homeowners should also note that ERCOT grid interconnection paperwork, required when adding solar or battery backup, presumes a panel rated for modern fault-current interruption. Older breaker boxes frequently fail this review, forcing an unplanned upgrade mid-project. Budgeting for a panel replacement alongside any major electrification investment – rather than treating it as deferrable maintenance – consistently produces the smallest total cost outcome in Texas market data.

What It Means for Homeowners

For Texas homeowners weighing a panel upgrade, the data points to a clear inflection: if your breaker box predates 1990, lacks AFCI protection, or shows signs of heat damage, the cost of inaction now outweighs the cost of replacement. The 2026 National Electrical Code, which Texas adopted statewide, requires arc-fault protection on 120-volt, 15- and 20-amp branch circuits supplying most living areas, including bedrooms, living rooms, kitchens, and hallways, and on existing circuits in those areas when they are modified, replaced, or extended. Older Federal Pacific and Zinsco panels, still common in homes built between 1950 and 1985, cannot accept a modern arc fault breaker or arc detector breaker without full replacement.

The practical takeaway: budget between $2,500 and $4,500 for a standard 200-amp service upgrade in Texas, with AFCI breaker retrofits adding $40 to $80 per circuit. Homes with electric vehicle chargers, heat pumps, or solar interconnections will trend toward the higher end. Whole-home surge protection is more than an add-on: since the 2020 edition, the NEC (230.67) requires a surge protective device when a dwelling service is installed or replaced, typically $300 to $600 installed.

Call a licensed Texas electrician immediately if you notice: breakers that trip repeatedly, scorch marks or a burning smell near the panel, lights that dim when large appliances cycle on, or a panel that feels warm to the touch. These are leading indicators of arc fault conditions that an arc fault interrupter breaker is designed to detect before ignition occurs.

Verify your contractor holds a current TDLR Master Electrician license, pulls a municipal permit, and schedules the required AHJ inspection before energizing the new service.

Texas homeowners weighing a breaker box upgrade should factor in code-required AFCI breaker coverage – also called arc fault circuit interrupter breakers or arc detector breakers – across most living-area circuits.
