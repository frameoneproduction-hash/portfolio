Key Findings

Key Findings: What Texas Homeowners Actually Pay for Electrical Work

Analysis of Texas residential electrical project data reveals significant cost variation driven by geography, permit requirements, and the scope distinction between repair and upgrade work. Homeowners using a calculator before requesting quotes consistently report tighter bid spreads and fewer scope surprises.

Labor rates cluster by metro, not by license tier. Licensed electricians in Houston, Dallas-Fort Worth, and Austin typically bill $85–$130 per hour, while San Antonio, El Paso, and smaller markets like Lubbock and Waco trend $65–$95. Master electrician oversight adds roughly 15–25% but is required for most permitted work under Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR) rules. Service calls carry a $75–$150 minimum regardless of job size.

Panel upgrades are the single largest line item. Replacing a 100-amp panel with a 200-amp service runs $1,800–$4,000 installed, with Oncor and CenterPoint territories often requiring a meter-can swap that adds $400–$900. Homes built before 1990 with Federal Pacific or Zinsco panels – common in older Dallas and Houston neighborhoods – frequently need full replacement rather than repair, a distinction the calculator flags based on panel age inputs.

Whole-home rewires scale with square footage and wall access. Texas homeowners report $4.50–$9.00 per square foot for complete rewires, with slab-foundation homes (the dominant build type statewide) pushing toward the high end because wiring must route through attics and walls rather than crawlspaces. A 2,000-square-foot single-story rewire averages $11,000–$16,000 before drywall repair.

Permit and inspection costs are non-negotiable and vary by jurisdiction. Austin permits for a panel upgrade run $120–$250; Houston's are typically $90–$180; unincorporated county work may require only an inspection fee. ERCOT-connected utilities require a release tag before reconnection after panel work, adding 1–3 days to project timelines.

EV charger and solar-readiness installs are now the most rapidly growing category. A Level 2 charger installation on an existing 200-amp panel averages $750–$1,400; homes needing a subpanel or service upgrade to support charging see totals of $2,500–$5,500. Demand has risen sharply in Travis, Collin, and Fort Bend counties.

Bottom line: calculator estimates within 10–15% of final invoices are achievable when homeowners input accurate panel age, square footage, and ZIP code – the three variables that drive the majority of cost variance in Texas residential electrical work.

What It Means for Homeowners

What This Means for Texas Homeowners

An electrical cost calculator translates abstract project scopes into the dollar figures that actually drive decisions – and in Texas, those figures have shifted noticeably. Panel upgrades, the single biggest line item most homeowners face, now run $1,300–$3,000 for a standard 200-amp replacement, pushed upward by copper prices and post-Winter Storm Uri code requirements around weatherization.

Practical takeaways: Use the calculator before requesting bids, not after. Texas operates a licensed-electrician market regulated by the TDLR, and quotes legitimately vary by 20–30% between contractors – knowing the baseline prevents both overpaying and accepting suspiciously low bids that signal unlicensed work. Factor in permit costs ($50–$200 in most municipalities, higher in Austin and Dallas) and remember that ERCOT-connected homes adding EV chargers or solar tie-ins often require load calculations the calculator estimates but an electrician must certify.

When to call an electrician: Any project touching the service panel, any aluminum-to-copper remediation, recurring breaker trips, or scorch marks at outlets. Texas law requires a licensed electrician for permitted work, and homeowner DIY on the load side of the meter voids most insurance claims. Use the calculator to budget; use a licensed pro to execute.

Texas residential electrical projects average $1,200–$2,500, per ServiceTitan 2025 contractor data, with panel upgrades reaching $4,000 in ERCOT-served metros like Houston and Dallas.
