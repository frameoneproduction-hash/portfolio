Key Findings

Key Findings: What Texas Homeowners Actually Pay for Lighting Installation

Analysis of recent Texas residential electrical pricing data reveals that lighting installation costs in 2026 vary more by metro region and fixture complexity than by any other factor. Statewide, homeowners report paying between $150 and $980 per fixture installed, with the median single-fixture job landing at roughly $280 – a figure that includes labor, basic materials, and standard permitting where applicable.

Labor rates drive the largest cost variance. Licensed Texas electricians currently bill between $75 and $135 per hour, according to Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR) wage surveys and contractor listings. Austin and the Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex sit at the high end of that range ($110–$135/hr), while San Antonio, El Paso, and most rural counties cluster between $75 and $95/hr. Houston straddles the middle at roughly $95–$120/hr, reflecting post-hurricane demand for licensed work.

Fixture type is the second-largest variable. Replacing an existing fixture on an active circuit averages 45–75 minutes of labor – typically $90 to $170 all-in. Installing a new fixture where no wiring exists requires running cable, cutting drywall, and adding a junction box, pushing jobs into the $350–$750 range per location. Recessed can lights (LED retrofits) average $125–$250 each when installed in batches of four or more; one-off cans run 30–40% higher per unit. Ceiling fan installations with light kits average $200–$450, with vaulted-ceiling work adding $100–$200 for scaffolding or lift time.

Permits and inspections matter in Texas. Major metros – Austin, Houston, Dallas, San Antonio, and Plano – require permits for new circuits, exterior fixtures, and most landscape lighting installations. Permit fees range from $35 to $180 per project, and inspection delays of 5–10 business days are common in Travis and Harris counties.

Smart and specialty lighting carry a premium. Adding dimmer switches averages $85–$165 installed; smart switches with neutral-wire requirements run $140–$240. Whole-home lighting control systems (Lutron Caséta, Control4) range from $1,800 to $9,500 depending on scope.

Homeowners who bundle multiple fixtures in a single service call reduce per-fixture cost by an average of 22%, since the trip charge ($65–$125 across most Texas providers) is absorbed once. Obtaining three written bids – a practice TDLR explicitly recommends – typically reveals price spreads of 30–40% on identical scopes of work.

What It Means for Homeowners

What This Means for Texas Homeowners

The numbers behind lighting installation in Texas tell a clear story: labor, not fixtures, drives most project budgets. Licensed Texas electricians typically bill $75–$125 per hour, with the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR) requiring licensure for nearly all permanent wiring work. A calculator-based estimate helps homeowners separate the $40 recessed can from the $180 it costs to cut drywall, run Romex through insulated attic space, and tie into an existing circuit in a Houston or Dallas home built before the 2008 NEC tamper-resistant requirements.

Practical takeaways: expect recessed lighting retrofits to run $200–$350 per fixture installed, ceiling fan and light combos $150–$400 depending on whether a box and switch leg already exist, and chandelier swaps under 50 pounds to stay near $150 when the junction box is rated. Texas's climate matters too – attic work between June and September often carries a premium because crews limit exposure when attic temperatures exceed 130°F, a documented OSHA heat-illness threshold.

When to call an electrician: any project that adds a new circuit, modifies the service panel, or installs lighting in wet locations (bathrooms, covered patios, pool areas) falls under the National Electrical Code and Texas Occupations Code Chapter 1305, which restricts this work to licensed professionals. Homeowners can legally replace a like-for-like fixture on a de-energized circuit, but if the existing box is plastic, ungrounded, or shows scorch marks – common in Texas homes wired before 1975 – stop and schedule an inspection before the swap.

Data & methodology

Cost estimates in this calculator draw from a blend of Texas-specific and national data sources, refreshed quarterly. Labor rates are derived from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics (OEWS) for Texas electricians (SOC 47-2111), which reported a mean hourly wage of $25.18 statewide as of May 2024, with metropolitan adjustments applied for Houston, Dallas–Fort Worth, Austin, San Antonio, and El Paso. Permit fees reflect schedules published by municipal building departments, including the City of Austin Development Services Department and Houston's Permitting Center, where residential electrical permits typically range from $50 to $175 depending on circuit count.

Fixture and materials pricing is sampled monthly from regional supplier catalogs and major retailers serving Texas markets. We cross-reference Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR) requirements for licensed electrician involvement, which directly affect labor line items. Estimates assume compliance with the 2023 National Electrical Code as adopted by Texas. Figures are statewide averages; actual quotes vary by home age, panel capacity, and local cost of living.
