Allen Electrical Questions, Answered

What should I do if I notice a burning smell near an outlet in my Allen home?
A burning smell can mean overheating wiring, a failing receptacle, or a loose connection inside the box. In Allen, storm activity and heavy cooling loads can both make a weak electrical point show itself faster. Unplug nearby devices only if you can do it safely, avoid touching a warm or discolored outlet, and do not keep using that circuit. If the odor continues, you see smoke, or the panel is buzzing, call dispatch immediately.
After a storm in Allen, how do I know whether a partial outage is a utility issue or a home electrical problem?
A partial outage may come from the utility side, but it can also point to damage at your panel, meter equipment, service connection, or an internal circuit. Allen customers should check a current bill or the outage map for the utility serving their address. Oncor serves much of the area, but not every home. If neighbors have power, only part of your house is out, lights are unusually dim, or breakers will not stay on, do not keep toggling breakers. Call and describe what is happening.
Why does my breaker trip when the AC starts in Allen?
A breaker that trips when the AC starts may be reacting to startup load, a weak breaker, a shorted component, or a circuit that is no longer handling demand safely. Allen’s summer heat and high use of electric systems can put repeated stress on panels and HVAC circuits. You can note whether the trip happens instantly or after several minutes, but do not upsize the breaker or reset it repeatedly. Call before the problem turns into heat, odor, or equipment damage.
Are flickering lights after hail or wind something to call about?
Flickering after weather can be as simple as utility instability, but it can also mean a loose connection, damaged service equipment, or a circuit problem inside the home. Allen has meaningful hail and storm exposure, so new flicker after a storm deserves attention. Check whether the flicker affects the whole house or one room, and look for buzzing, heat, or burning odor. Do not ignore flicker that gets worse or happens with crackling sounds; call dispatch.
What if an outdoor outlet trips after rain in Allen?
An outdoor outlet that trips after rain may be reacting to moisture in the receptacle, a failed cover, damaged wiring, or a GFCI doing its job. Allen storms can bring rain, wind, and debris that expose weak outdoor electrical points. Keep the area dry if you can do so without touching wet equipment, leave the outlet off, and do not bypass the GFCI. If the reset will not hold or the outlet feels warm, call dispatch.
Should I call if my Allen panel is buzzing or crackling?
Buzzing or crackling from a panel can indicate arcing, a loose connection, failing equipment, or an overloaded circuit. That is not normal household noise, even in a newer home. Stand back from the panel, avoid removing the cover, and do not keep cycling breakers to see what happens. If the sound is active, especially with odor, heat, or flickering lights, call dispatch immediately.
Can a surge after a storm damage outlets or appliances in Allen?
Surge symptoms can show up as failed electronics, tripped breakers, dead outlets, or lights that behave strangely after weather. In Allen, frequent hail and storm activity make post-storm electrical symptoms worth taking seriously. Unplug sensitive equipment if it is safe, check whether multiple rooms are affected, and do not keep using outlets that spark or smell hot. Call dispatch when the symptoms involve more than one device, a breaker will not hold, or anything feels warm.
Why does power go out in one room but the rest of my Allen house works?
One-room power loss often points to a tripped breaker, a failed GFCI, a damaged device, or a loose connection on that branch circuit. In a newer Allen home, the cause may still be hidden behind a receptacle, switch, or junction point. Check for a clearly tripped breaker or GFCI once, but do not force a reset if it trips again. Call when the outage returns, the outlet is warm, or the room lost power after plugging in an appliance.
