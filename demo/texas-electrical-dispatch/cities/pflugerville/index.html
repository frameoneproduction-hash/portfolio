Pflugerville Electrical Questions, Answered

Why do my lights flicker when the AC starts in my Pflugerville home?
Brief dimming can happen when a large motor starts, but repeated flicker, breaker trips, buzzing, or lights that stay dim may point to a circuit, panel, or service issue. Pflugerville homes often rely heavily on electric cooling during hot weather, so AC-start symptoms are common enough to take seriously. Note whether the flicker affects one room or the whole house, but do not keep cycling the system to test it. Call if flicker is new, worsening, or paired with heat, odor, or tripping.
Is a partial outage after a Pflugerville storm a utility problem or an electrician problem?
A storm-related partial outage may come from the grid, damaged service equipment, a panel issue, or a failed connection feeding part of the home. Pflugerville has meaningful hail and flood-related storm signals, so timing after weather matters. Check neighbors and confirm your provider on your bill or an outage map, but do not open the panel or touch wet equipment. Call if only your home is affected, some rooms are out, lights are unusually bright or dim, or you smell heat.
Should I call immediately for a burning smell near a switch or outlet?
Yes. A burning smell can mean overheating, arcing, a failing device, or a damaged connection behind the wall. Even though Pflugerville's housing stock is newer than many Texas cities, newer homes can still have overloaded devices, loose terminations, or damaged equipment. Stop using the switch or outlet and turn off the circuit only if it is safe and clearly identified. Do not keep testing it with lamps or appliances; call immediately if odor remains, the cover is warm, or there is smoke.
What does it mean when a breaker trips whenever the AC comes on?
That pattern may involve compressor startup load, a weak breaker, a circuit problem, or equipment drawing more current than expected. Pflugerville's heat and cooling demand make AC-related trips especially disruptive, but the breaker is still a warning device. Replace or clean filters only as normal HVAC maintenance, and leave electrical testing to a pro. Do not install a larger breaker, bypass the trip, or keep resetting it through the afternoon; call when it repeats.
Can hail damage cause surge symptoms inside the house?
Hail and wind can damage exterior equipment, and storm activity can also coincide with surge symptoms such as failed electronics, tripped breakers, or flickering lights. Pflugerville's local storm profile includes a high hail signal, so post-storm changes deserve attention. Unplug sensitive equipment if it is safe, look for visible damage from a distance, and avoid touching service lines or wet devices. Call if breakers trip, outlets stop working, or the panel shows noise, odor, or heat.
Why will my GFCI outlet not reset after rain or bathroom use?
A GFCI that will not reset may be detecting moisture, a ground fault, downstream wiring trouble, or a failed device. In Pflugerville, storm runoff and humid wet areas can make outdoor, garage, kitchen, and bath GFCI complaints more urgent when water is involved. Unplug connected devices and let wet areas stay off. Do not bypass the GFCI or replace it blindly if the circuit may still be wet or damaged; call if it trips again or will not hold reset.
When should I worry about lights dimming when appliances start?
Dimming that happens only for a split second may be normal, but repeated dimming across the home can suggest voltage drop, loose connections, overloaded circuits, or service issues. Pflugerville's modern housing does not rule out load problems, especially where HVAC, kitchen appliances, and home-office equipment run together. Track which appliance causes it and whether the dimming affects one area or the whole house. Do not ignore dimming with buzzing, warm outlets, or burning odor; call when those symptoms appear.
Is it safe to use a generator during a Pflugerville outage?
A generator can help during an outage, but it must be isolated from the utility system with proper transfer equipment. In storm-related outages, rushed hookups create serious risks for the home and for utility crews. Keep portable generators outdoors and away from openings, and use only properly rated connections. Do not backfeed through a dryer outlet or improvise a cord setup; call if you need safe transfer-switch help or see electrical symptoms when power returns.
