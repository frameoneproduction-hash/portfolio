Key Findings

Key Findings: What House Rewiring Actually Costs in Texas

Texas homeowners researching rewiring costs in 2026 face a wide pricing band, and the variance is driven less by region than by three measurable variables: home square footage, accessibility of existing wiring, and the scope of panel work bundled into the job.

Statewide cost range. Full rewires in Texas currently run between $4.20 and $9.80 per square foot of conditioned living space. For the median Texas single-family home at roughly 2,100 square feet, that translates to a typical project cost of $8,800 to $20,500. Partial rewires – limited to a kitchen, bathroom circuit, or knob-and-tube replacement in a single zone – generally fall between $1,500 and $4,500.

Age of housing stock is the dominant cost driver. Roughly 18% of Texas homes were built before 1970, concentrated in Houston's Heights, San Antonio's Monte Vista, Dallas's M Streets, and older Austin neighborhoods east of I-35. These homes frequently contain aluminum branch wiring (1965–1973 vintage), cloth-insulated conductors, or ungrounded two-prong circuits – all of which increase labor hours by 30% to 60% compared with a straightforward copper-on-copper replacement.

Panel upgrades add a predictable premium. A rewire paired with a service upgrade from 100A to 200A adds $2,200 to $3,800 in most Texas markets. Utility coordination fees with Oncor, CenterPoint, AEP Texas, or CPS Energy add another $200 to $600 depending on whether a meter pull and re-inspection are required. Homes with Federal Pacific Stab-Lok or Zinsco panels – still common in 1960s–70s Texas builds – should budget for mandatory replacement; insurers including State Farm and Farmers have flagged these panels as non-renewable risks in Texas policies since 2023.

Permit and inspection costs. Texas does not issue rewiring permits at the state level; costs vary by AHJ. Houston permits average $165 for residential electrical work, Dallas runs $135–$190, Austin $145, and unincorporated county jobs typically require only a TDLR-licensed electrician's sign-off with no municipal fee.

Code-driven scope creep. Any rewire triggers 2023 NEC compliance for the affected circuits – meaning AFCI protection on bedroom and living-area circuits, GFCI on all kitchen, bath, laundry, garage, and exterior outlets, and tamper-resistant receptacles throughout. Homeowners should expect device costs alone to add $400–$900 to estimates that quote only labor and wire.

What It Means for Homeowners

What This Means for Texas Homeowners

A house rewiring estimator is more than a budgeting exercise in Texas – it's a risk calculation. The state's housing stock skews older than many realize: U.S. Census data shows roughly a third of Texas homes were built before 1980, the era when aluminum branch wiring, ungrounded two-prong outlets, and undersized 60-amp panels were common. Combined with the grid stress exposed by Winter Storm Uri in February 2021 and the rising electrical load from EV chargers, heat pumps, and battery backups, accurate cost forecasting has become a practical necessity rather than a luxury.

Practical takeaways. Most Texas rewires fall between $8,000 and $20,000 for a 1,500–3,000 sq ft home, with costs driven by square footage, wall accessibility, panel upgrades (often $2,000–$4,500 on their own), and permit fees that vary widely between Houston, Austin, Dallas, and unincorporated counties. Estimator outputs should be treated as planning ranges, not bids – humidity, slab foundations common in Texas, and attic temperatures regularly exceeding 140°F all affect labor hours. Budget an additional 10–15% contingency for drywall repair, which estimators frequently exclude.

When to call an electrician. Stop using the estimator as a substitute for inspection if you notice warm outlets, frequent breaker trips, flickering lights tied to appliance cycles, a burning odor, or a Federal Pacific or Zinsco panel. Texas law requires a licensed electrical contractor (TDLR-licensed) for rewiring work, and most insurers will deny claims tied to unpermitted electrical fires. Schedule a load assessment before any major remodel or solar interconnection.

Data & methodology

Cost estimates in this tool draw on a blend of public and industry data sources calibrated to Texas residential conditions. Baseline labor rates reference the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics for electricians in Texas metropolitan areas (Houston, Dallas-Fort Worth, Austin, San Antonio, and El Paso), where mean hourly wages typically run below the national average but vary by metro. Material pricing reflects rolling averages from major Texas-based supply distributors and national wholesalers, adjusted quarterly for copper commodity fluctuations tracked through COMEX futures.

Permit fee inputs are sourced directly from municipal building department schedules, including Austin Development Services, Houston Public Works, and Dallas Building Inspection. All work assumed in estimates conforms to the 2023 National Electrical Code as adopted by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation, which licenses electricians statewide.

Square footage ranges, panel amperage assumptions, and circuit counts are modeled against typical Texas housing stock data from the U.S. Census American Community Survey, weighted toward homes built between 1970 and 2000 – the population most likely to require full rewiring today.
