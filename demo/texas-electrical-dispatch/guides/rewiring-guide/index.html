Whole House Rewiring Guide Texas

Whole house rewiring in Texas typically runs $8,000 to $20,000 for a 2,000-square-foot home, with costs climbing higher in older Houston, Galveston, and San Antonio properties where knob-and-tube or aluminum branch wiring remains. This guide examines when full rewiring becomes unavoidable – frequent breaker trips, two-prong outlets, cloth-insulated conductors, or homes built before 1972 – and walks through permit requirements under the National Electrical Code adopted statewide by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (the 2026 edition since September 1, 2026). It covers Master Electrician licensing verification, AFCI and GFCI mandates, panel upgrades to 200-amp service, and timelines averaging five to ten business days. Key takeaway: Texas homeowners should budget for permit fees, secure at least three licensed bids, and prioritize rewiring before adding EV chargers or solar interconnections, since ERCOT-tied load demands strain outdated systems.

Key Findings

Whole-house rewiring in Texas typically runs $8,000 to $20,000 for a 2,000-square-foot home, before permits, drywall repair, and any panel work are added. Per-square-foot pricing generally falls between $4 and $9, though Austin, Dallas, and Houston metros often trend toward the upper end due to labor demand. Older Texas housing stock drives much of this work: roughly 18% of Texas housing units were built before 1970, according to U.S. Census American Community Survey data, and many older homes still have cloth-insulated conductors or ungrounded two-prong circuits, while homes built or rewired from about 1965 to the mid-1970s may have aluminum branch wiring – none of which meets current National Electrical Code requirements for new work.

The clearest signals you need a full rewire rather than spot repairs include a Federal Pacific Stab-Lok or Zinsco panel (both linked to elevated fire risk and routinely flagged by Texas home inspectors), recurring breaker trips on general lighting circuits, warm or discolored outlets, and any knob-and-tube or ungrounded wiring discovered during a real-estate transaction.

Timeline expectations matter. A licensed Texas Master Electrician working with one or two journeymen typically completes a single-story rewire in 5 to 10 working days; two-story homes with finished ceilings stretch to 10 to 15 days because of additional fishing and drywall patching. Permits are mandatory in every incorporated Texas city and most ETJ areas, and the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR) requires that the contractor – not the homeowner – pull the electrical permit. Verifying a TDLR license number before signing is the single most effective fraud-prevention step; the agency's public license search returns active status, disciplinary history, and bond information in seconds.

Homeowners should also budget for upgrades often done alongside a rewire: a 200-amp service panel ($2,500–$4,500 installed), whole-home surge protection ($300–$600, which the NEC has required on dwelling services since the 2020 edition), AFCI and GFCI protection on new circuits where code calls for it, and tamper-resistant receptacles. Bundling these during the rewire avoids a second permit cycle and usually costs less than doing the same work in phases.

What It Means for Homeowners

The price reflects more than copper and conduit: Texas adopted the 2026 National Electrical Code (NEC) statewide effective September 1, 2026, replacing the 2023 edition, meaning any rewire must incorporate AFCI protection in most living areas, GFCI coverage in kitchens and bathrooms, and tamper-resistant receptacles throughout. Homes built before 1975 – a significant share of housing stock in older neighborhoods like Oak Cliff, the Heights, and East Austin – often contain aluminum branch wiring or cloth-insulated copper, both flagged by the Consumer Product Safety Commission as elevated fire risks.

Practical takeaways: rewiring is rarely a weekend project. Expect 3–10 days of work, partial loss of power, and drywall repair costs that many quotes exclude. Texas law (Occupations Code Chapter 1305) requires the work to be performed by electricians licensed by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR) working for a licensed electrical contractor, apart from a narrow owner-occupant exemption, and a permit is mandatory in nearly every municipality.

Call a licensed electrician immediately if you notice recurring breaker trips, warm outlets, flickering lights across multiple circuits, a burning-plastic odor, or two-prong ungrounded receptacles. Schedule an inspection before purchase on any Texas home older than 40 years – the diagnostic visit typically costs $150–$350 and can prevent five-figure surprises.
